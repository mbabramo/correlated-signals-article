# Signal-diagram family

The current publication candidates are generated by ACESim4's C# LitigCharts
project. Each has matching `-color` and `-bw` PDF, PNG, self-contained TeX,
description (.txt), and unrounded probability-flow data (.json).

| Model prefix | Separate relationship files | Intended use |
|---|---|---|
| Continuous merits | truth, party, court | Party diagram provisionally main Figure 1; truth and court Appendix A |
| Truth-conditioned merits | truth (truth to discrete merits), party, court | Appendix B |
| Direct binary signals | party, court | Appendix B |

## Reading the diagrams

Ribbon widths are **joint probability masses**. In color, lower-half source buckets
are orange and upper-half buckets are blue (a bucket centered exactly at the midpoint
joins the upper half). One reference fan retains its source hue at greater saturation,
in front of the pale background flows. In black and white,
all background ribbons have the same pale gray fill,
regardless of signal strength or source range. Only the reference fan uses darker
gray, drawn last. No ribbons have outlines, including the highlighted fan: stroke
width cannot inflate small probability flows, and no white ribbon borders cut
across previously drawn flows. Gray shade indicates highlighting, not
signal strength; the same highlighting is available in both palettes.
The reference fan's **conditional** destination
probabilities are saved in the matching TXT and JSON files, in bottom-to-top
order. Each is the joint mass of a ribbon divided by the total mass of the
reference source bucket, so the conditional probabilities sum to one. In the
party diagram these are destination signal-bin probabilities, not probabilities
of an exact midpoint. Numeric bucket labels identify those bin midpoints.

The graphics contain only short column and bucket labels: no sentence title or
bottom explanation. Q and T use mathematical italics, and the noise standard
deviation uses the Greek symbol sigma rather than the spelled-out word.

The baseline groups continuous Q into five display intervals. The generator
integrates the actual production signal kernel within each interval, rather
than evaluating it at a midpoint. The intervals are not model states or
quadrature nodes. Signals are independent conditional on exact Q, but not
generally conditional on a displayed interval. The reference Q interval
0.60--0.80 has a 0.70 chance of true liability and approximately a 0.82 chance
of a liable court finding: truth and adjudication are distinct.

The party diagram represents either party because their noise is equal in these
specifications. It does not show an observed opponent signal. Court findings
are not observed by litigants before bargaining. The separate diagrams depict marginal
distributions, not successive information revealed during play.

For direct binary signals, the calibrated production sigmas are approximately
0.349828304 for parties and 0.3060453855 for the court; the displayed headers
round these values. The truth-conditioned model uses a truth-to-merits sigma
of 0.35 and party/court sigma of 0.2.

## Regeneration

From ACESim4, run as one command:

```text
dotnet run --project LitigCharts -c Release -- diagrams signals --config "C:/Users/Admin/source/repos/correlated-signals-article/article-diagrams.json"
```

The `all` target also includes this family. See LitigCharts/README.md for
sources-only, compile-only, output-root and jobs options. No solver or saved
equilibrium is needed for these probability diagrams.

There are eight forward relationships and sixteen forward PDFs (color and grayscale),
plus three inverse party-signal relationships in both palettes (six more PDFs),
and three party-to-party prediction relationships in both palettes (six more PDFs).
For example, `Continuous merits - party - bw.pdf` is the grayscale party-signal
illustration. No combined multi-panel PDF is generated or retained.

## Inverse diagrams: party signal to merits or truth

Use `diagrams inverse-signals --config <article-diagrams.json>` to regenerate:

- `Continuous merits - party inverse - bw.pdf` (and `-color.pdf`).
- `Truth-conditioned merits - party inverse - bw.pdf` (and `-color.pdf`).
- `Direct binary signals - party inverse - bw.pdf` (and `-color.pdf`).

The same lower-middle signal bin, labeled 0.45, is the reference in all three,
highlighted in both palettes. Its
0.55 counterpart is the mirror image. The two merits destination axes use the same five
merits intervals; each interval in the truth-conditioned model collects two
discrete merits levels, without smoothing them. Joint flows are transposed
from the forward distributions with their prior weights intact. TXT/JSON
companions give the normalized posterior probabilities conditional on that one
signal bin, before any procedural selection. These are not posterior beliefs
conditioned on observed filing, answering, or an opponent signal.

At the production calibration, the central 0.40--0.60 posterior mass is about
34.8% for continuous merits and 31.4% for truth-conditioned merits. The comparison
is therefore modest; these diagrams do not show a dramatic split or two sharply
different middle-signal distributions. The direct-binary inverse instead has
the two truth states as destinations, with posterior probabilities about 60.0%
not liable and 40.0% liable. Its middle signal represents uncertainty about truth,
not intermediate latent merits. Both the baseline and this alternative have
binary truth; the distinction is the presence of shared merits heterogeneity,
including within a true-liability state. These are additional explanatory candidates, not an
automatic change to the four-figure main-article plan.

The configuration's SignalSpecifications list can also select LowNoise,
HighNoise, CenterWeightedContinuousMerits or PolarizedContinuousMerits.
Those draw from the corresponding production settings. They are not selected
by default: keep the main article focused and request additional supplementary
illustrations when needed.

Damages remain available via the separate `damages-signals` target, routed
outside this article by the configuration. The current continuous-merits
implementation uses fixed damages, so that legacy extension is not an article
figure.

## Party signal to other-party signal

Use `diagrams party-to-party --config <article-diagrams.json>` to regenerate
the three standalone `party to party` diagrams in color and grayscale. They are
also included in `all`. The diagrams use the production joint signal distribution,
not a product of average signal kernels over coarse merits intervals. One reference
plaintiff signal (0.45) predicts the distribution of possible defendant signals;
it does not reveal the opponent's signal or cause it. With equal party noise the
same relationship applies to the defendant's prediction of the plaintiff signal.
No filing, answering, offers, or trial selection is conditioned on.

The matching TXT companions show both the unconditional and conditional distributions.
For opponent signals in [0.40,0.60), combining the bins labeled 0.45 and 0.55:

| Model | Unconditional probability | Given own signal 0.45 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Continuous merits | 21.5% | 27.8% |
| Truth-conditioned merits | 20.1% | 26.7% |
| Direct binary signals | 16.7% | 16.7% |

The comparison uses each production calibration; party noise is not held identical
across models. However, the absence of an update about the opponent's distance
from the midpoint in the symmetric direct-binary model is structural: a symmetric
range has equal likelihood under both truth states. Own information changes the
mixing weights on those states but cannot change that range's probability. It
can predict which side the opponent is likely to occupy. The other two models
allow a middle signal to convey information about shared intermediate merits.

This sharpens the response to the editor without claiming that binary models
cannot yield uncertain liability beliefs or close cases in that sense. Binary
truth is present in the baseline too; the distinguishing restriction is independent
signal noise conditional only on truth, with no shared within-truth merits variation.
These explanatory candidates do not automatically change the four main figures.

## Publication and packaging

All ribbons use opaque flat fills, with no gradients or alpha transparency.
The grayscale files use uniform pale-gray background ribbons plus one darker
reference fan. All ribbons are fill-only, without outlines. Bucket outlines are black.
There is no shade scale based on signal strength. The companion TXT/JSON reference
probabilities describe the highlighted fan in both palettes.
Fonts are embedded. Preserve vector PDF inclusion; inspect the final
submission-system proof as well as the local manuscript.

The earlier `Truth-conditioned merits - party signals.pdf` is retained only
as a historical source for comparing the original rendering with the distorted
submission proof. It is superseded for publication by the new standalone relationship files.
The other historical variants remain recoverable from Git.

The signal PDFs are research/publication candidates in this folder; publication
copies will be frozen in Figures only after figure selection and manuscript
layout review. Every relationship is a separate PDF, not a panel in a combined file. Any later
composition is a manuscript-layout choice. Do not shrink the diagrams until
labels become too small to read.
